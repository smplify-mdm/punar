The desktop is the sheet a plate is drawn on, not a picture hung on a wall.

Punar’s background is the same instrument that draws the boot ring — a sixty-slot dial, four concentric hairlines, dashed radials, one datum point — printed on the field at watermark contrast. Two five-kilobyte SVGs, paper and panel, one construction. It is the only surface in the system that asserts nothing, and that is exactly why it can be everywhere.

Sect I · Motif register

The wallpaper is the drawing the plates are drawn on — the one Punar surface that asserts nothing.

01Lineage

The boot dial in Plate D-002 is sixty ticks with uneven emphasis, a progress arc drawn clockwise from twelve, and a solid centre dot. The wallpaper is that same construction with the arc removed — because an idle desktop is not doing anything, and a ring that implied progress would be a claim. Boot finishes, the arc dissolves, the instrument stays as the field. design language §7 · Plate D-002

02Construction

Four concentric hairlines at r 208 / 160 / 108 / 56; a sixty-slot tick ring on the rim; eight dashed radials offset 22.5° so they never lie on the centre lines; ISO chain lines through the datum, bleeding off every edge; one filled datum point at the origin. No stations, no numbered mechanisms, no annotations — nothing that a register below could name. design language §3

03No claim strokes

§7 gives stroke a meaning: solid marks an operating production path, dashed marks a mechanism outside the current claim. The wallpaper annotates no mechanism, so it is drawn entirely in construction-line vocabulary — the dashed radials are drafting construction, not unshipped futures. The rule this plate adds: if the field ever carries an annotated element it must obey §7 in full, so it never will. design language §7 · spec §1.22

04The rim

The sixty slots spell PUNAR in Morse, starting at twelve and running clockwise: one slot per symbol, one blank slot between symbols, three between letters. Long tick is a dash, medium tick a dot, plain hairline is silence; slots 35–59 are silent. It sits at 1.14:1 against paper and 1.22:1 against panel, which is why it is allowed to exist — §7 permits the rim to reward close reading, never at the cost of legibility. design language §7

05One field, no org

There is no managed wallpaper and there never will be. Enrollment is additive chrome on panels and mastheads; it does not get to touch the desktop field, brand it, or watermark it. A personal machine and an enrolled machine show the identical background. design language §8 · spec §3.2

Sect II · Legibility register

A background that changes what anything on top of it means is not a background.

01Contrast ceiling

Paper: hairline #F4F2EC at 1.06:1 and emphasis #EDEAE2 at 1.14:1 against #FAF9F6. Panel has no token between surface and edge, so both tones are #26282E at two opacities — 0.42 gives 1.10:1 and 0.75 gives 1.22:1 against #08090A. The rule: no wallpaper stroke may exceed 1.25:1 against its own field. design language §2 · §9.5

02The border wins

Every wallpaper stroke is strictly lighter than the 1px window-border token — #E6E4DE reads 1.21:1 on paper, #26282E reads 1.35:1 on panel. In relative luminance the border’s step is 1.38× (paper) and 1.58× (panel) the motif’s darkest step. Edge contrast, not ink area, decides which line reads as a boundary, and contrast is a colour fact independent of resolution. design language §3

03Text on top

Ink on bare paper measures 19.95:1 and falls to 17.47:1 where it crosses the darkest stroke. Panel-fg on bare panel measures 17.95:1 and falls to 14.69:1. The motif costs 12% of the ratio on paper and 18% on panel, and neither crosses a class boundary — everything stays above AAA’s 7:1, four times AA’s 4.5:1. State 01 and 02 put live type on the motif so this is checked by eye, not asserted. design language §9.5 · spec §12

04The bar is opaque

The shell bar is a filled surface rectangle closed by a hairline (Bar.qml: color: Theme.paperSurface), so the field passes behind it and never through it. The wallpaper cannot change one number in the bar’s contrast budget, and state 03 draws it that way. design language §5

05Acceptance

Every number above was measured, not asserted: the two SVGs were rendered at 3840×2400 and every pixel histogrammed, giving a worst stroke of #EDEAE2 at 1.142:1 on paper and rgb(30,32,36) at 1.222:1 on panel. The same check is headless and cheap — screenshot the desktop, histogram it, assert the darkest pixel that is not a window sits at or below 1.25:1 against the field. It rides the existing desktop screenshot job; no new harness. spec §12

Sect III · Geometry register

Fit, never crop — because the field is one flat colour, letterboxing is invisible.

01viewBox

viewBox="0 0 1600 1000" — a 16:10 reference sheet — with preserveAspectRatio="xMidYMid meet". The dial sits at (1152, 500): vertically centred, horizontally at 72%, outer radius 208. Vertical centring is not taste; it is what makes the worst crop harmless.

02Why meet, not slice

meet fits the whole drawing and letterboxes the remainder. Normally that is fatal for a wallpaper; here the field is a single flat token, so the bars are the same colour as the field and are literally invisible. The field rect overscans to 13600 × 9000 and the chain lines run from −6000 to 7600, and a root <svg> clips to its viewport rather than its viewBox — so the renderer paints those bars itself. Nothing is ever cropped, on any aspect ratio, including portrait.

03Aspect behaviour

The dial’s diameter is always 41.6% of the fitted height; its horizontal position is always 72% of the fitted width. The composition is a constant and only its scale moves. Even if a consumer ignores preserveAspectRatio and slices instead, every landscape ratio is still safe: 32:9 under slice leaves the band y ∈ [275, 725], and the dial spans [292, 708]. Only portrait needs meet.

04Nothing edge-critical

Every closed form — four circles, sixty ticks, the datum point — lives inside r 208 of the centre. The only geometry that touches an edge is a chain line, which is supposed to run off the sheet; a drafting centre line that ends inside the frame would be the error, not the bleed. design language §3

05Stroke scale

Widths are drawing units — 1 for hairlines, 1.5 for emphasis — and they scale with the output: 0.77 / 1.15 device px at 1366×768, 2.16 / 3.24 at 4K. Deliberate: this is a drawing, so it enlarges like one. UI hairlines stay 1 device pixel and still out-rank the field, because they out-rank it by colour and colour does not scale. Sect II · 02

Sect IV · Budget register

A background that costs five kilobytes cannot cost the image.

01Bytes

punar-wallpaper-paper.svg is 4,906 bytes and punar-wallpaper-panel.svg is 5,030 bytes. Of that, 2,508 and 2,569 bytes are geometry; the rest is the comment header that makes the file hand-checkable without a renderer. Together they are 9.7 KB of the image — less than one icon. PERFORMANCE_BUDGETS.md

02No raster

A PNG carrying this drawing at 4K would run into the hundreds of kilobytes and be wrong at every other resolution and every other aspect ratio; a JPEG would band a flat warm field visibly. Both rejected. The image ships one vector per surface and rasterizes it once, at the size the output actually is.

03No gradients, no fonts

No gradient, no filter, no mask, no <text>. The flat field is what makes the letterbox trick work, and the absence of text means the wallpaper renders identically before Instrument Sans and Geist Mono are loaded — which matters, because the background layer is up before the bar is. design language §1

04Zero idle cost

No animation, no script, no timer inside the asset, and no data behind it. It is rasterized once per output at startup and is a static texture thereafter, so its contribution to idle CPU is exactly zero — which is the only contribution §6.3 permits. spec §6.3 · PERFORMANCE_BUDGETS.md

Sect V · Implementation notes

The assets ship today; getting them on screen is a layer window, no new service and no new data.

01Data source: none

The wallpaper is the only shell surface with zero inputs. It does not read /run/punar/status.json, agents.json, or approvals.json; it holds no IPC subscription and no timer. Nothing the machine can observe is allowed to change the field — which is also why it can never lie. spec §1.22

02Choosing a variant

Honestly: there is no appearance preference anywhere in the tree today — Theme.qml reads punar-tokens.json and nothing else — so until one exists the shell hardcodes paper. The addition is ~/.config/punar/appearance.json  {"surface":"paper"|"panel"} followed by a FileView { watchChanges: true }, exactly the inotify pattern Status.qml already uses: event-driven, zero polling, fail-safe to paper when absent. No time-of-day switching — that needs a clock timer, and this plate refuses one. spec §6.3 · design language §6 M11

03Delivery — recommended

A Quickshell background layer window inside the existing punar-shell process: PanelWindow anchored on all four edges with WlrLayershell.layer: WlrLayer.Background, namespace punar-wallpaper, exclusionZone: 0, color: Theme.paperSurface, holding one Image { fillMode: PreserveAspectFit; sourceSize: Qt.size(width, height) }; a Variants over Quickshell.screens covers multi-monitor. Cost: no new process, no new unit, no new socket — one texture at output size (about 8 MB at 1080p, 33 MB at 4K) and a few kilobytes of parsed SVG. It needs the qtsvg image plugin present in the image; that is the one real packaging dependency this plate adds. M11

04Delivery — alternative

hyprpaper would mean a second daemon with its own RSS, its own IPC socket and its own unit, for a picture that never changes. Worse, its loader is bitmap-oriented: before choosing it, verify whether it can read SVG at all — if it cannot, the asset must be pre-rasterized per resolution at build or first boot, which throws away the resolution independence this whole plate is built on. Recommendation: the Quickshell layer window. spec §6.3

05Backstop

Set the compositor clear colour (misc:background_color) to the same token, so the gap between compositor start and shell start is the field colour and not black. Three independent mechanisms — the SVG’s own preserveAspectRatio, the QML fillMode, and the window’s color — all land on the same picture, and any two can fail.

06Milestone

The two SVGs are shippable now: static files, no code, no dependency on anything unbuilt. The layer window is design-ready, implementation M11 with the rest of the shell-services pass — it blocks nothing and nothing blocks it. It is deliberately not claimed for M1 or M2: the desktop is flat paper today and this page does not pretend otherwise. spec §76

07Not designed

Per-workspace fields, user-supplied images, a picker UI, and any animated variant are not designed and not planned. The field is a system constant; if that changes it earns its own plate.
